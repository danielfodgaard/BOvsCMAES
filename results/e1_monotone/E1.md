# E1: monotone transforms of f

Second run, 2026-11-06. The first run (seven transforms, two BO arms, BO budget 100) is frozen in
`results/e1_monotone_run1/`; its README lists what it changed here.

**What.** Run CMA-ES and BO on g(f) for strictly increasing g, and compare with the run on f under the same seed.
CMA-ES only uses the ranking of the f-values, so in theory it cannot tell g(f) from f. BO fits a GP to the
values, so it can.

**Setup.**
- Problem: H1's 5-D ellipsoid (condition number 100, x* in [−2, 2]⁵, box [−5, 5]⁵), seeds 0–9. The seed fixes x*,
  the CMA-ES start point and the Sobol initial design, so all three are the same for every g.
- g: f^p for p ∈ {1/4, 1/2, 1, 2, 4}; log f; the saturating f/(f + 100), where c = 100 is about the 1 % quantile of f
  over the box; and the control 1000·f. The control is affine, so by H1 it should have no effect: its gap is how far
  a BO result moves from rounding alone.
- CMA-ES: H1's runner (pycma 4.5.0, σ₀ = 2.5, popsize 8). *Fixed budget*: 2000 evaluations, f-based termination off.
  *Default stopping*: pycma's termination, capped at 2000 evaluations.
- BO: H1's runner (BoTorch 0.18.1, `SingleTaskGP` defaults: ARD RBF kernel, `Standardize`, `Normalize` on the inputs),
  LogEI, 10 Sobol points + 80 iterations. *Default*: the GP is fitted to g(f). *Ranks*: the GP is fitted to the ranks
  of g(f), recomputed every iteration (Shirakawa's fix). *Low noise floor*: the default with BoTorch's lower bound on
  the fitted noise variance moved from 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁸ (standardised units; on f the default bound is a noise std of
  about 6 in f units, 80× the best f the default reaches).
- Score: best **untransformed** f. Gap = log10 best f on g − log10 best f on f, same seed and arm; > 0 means worse on g.
  No absolute comparison between BO and CMA-ES: 90 and 2000 evaluations are different regimes.

**Result.** Median gap over 10 seeds (log10). In brackets: evaluations used by CMA-ES with default stopping.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| f (reference) | 0 | 0 (1288) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| log f | 0 | −7.7 (1880) | +2.1 | +2.9 | 0 |
| f^0.25 | 0 | −7.7 (1880) | +2.1 | +2.8 | 0 |
| f^0.5 | 0 | −7.7 (1880) | +1.5 | +2.4 | 0 |
| f^2 | 0 | +5.3 (864) | +2.6 | +2.9 | 0 |

Every 0 in the table is exact: the runs are bit-identical. Median best f on f after 90 evaluations: BO default 0.078,
BO low noise 0.014, BO ranks 5.9. The control's gaps lie in [−0.39, +0.20] (default) and [−0.97, +0.66] (low noise),
inside each arm's spread across seeds on f (0.64 and 1.33).

**Found.**
1. **CMA-ES's search is invariant; only its stopping rule is not.** With a fixed budget, the mean and σ of all 250
   generations are bit-identical for all eight g. Default stopping (`tolfun`, `tolfunhist`) compares *absolute*
   differences in g, so the run stops once the spread in f near the optimum drops below about 10⁻¹¹ / g′(f). This
   predicts the gap: −log10 1000 = −3 for 1000·f (observed −2.6) and +log10 100 = +2 for f/(f+100) (observed +2.1).
   f² and f⁴ stop 400–600 evaluations early, at f ≈ 10⁻⁹ and 10⁻⁶ instead of 5·10⁻¹⁵. Where g′ → ∞ at the optimum
   (p < 1 and log f), `tolfun` cannot fire before floating-point resolution, so the x-based `tolx` stops all three
   at the same point, later and better (f ≈ 10⁻²²); log f is indistinguishable from f^0.25 in every number.
   Any g with g′ → ∞ at the optimum defeats `tolfun`, so a user who takes the log of a cost gets a pycma run that
   quietly continues until `tolx`: longer, deeper, and probably more than they asked for. The gap column is
   deterministic, not a pattern to decode: fixed budget always reaches 10⁻²⁴, and default stopping only ever loses
   less or more against it depending on the sign of g′ at the optimum.
2. **Default BO is not invariant, and f itself is its best representation.** Every nonlinear g made BO worse, in all
   70 (g, seed) pairs, by +0.42 to +3.5. The control's gaps are inside the seed spread, so the effect comes from the
   nonlinearity, not from rounding; this is the separation H1 was meant to make possible. The damage is a stall, not a
   slow start: on every nonlinear g the median best-so-far curve follows the reference for the first 10–20 BO steps
   and then flattens (`best_so_far_bo.png`), under f⁴ at about 90 from evaluation 30 on.
3. **log f hurts as much as f^0.25.** Gap +2.1 (10/10 seeds), and its fitted noise is the highest of any transform
   (7·10⁻²). The prediction that it would help, as the compressing transform without a cusp, was wrong: log f has a
   singularity at the optimum, worse than the cusp of f^p, and the GP absorbs the pit as noise. So the one transform
   practitioners actually apply is the one that hurts default BO most among the compressing ones.
4. **The noise floor limits BO on f, but explains none of the transform gap.** Lowering the bound lets the fitted
   noise drop from 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁷ on f and 1000·f; best f improves from 0.078 to 0.014 (10/10 seeds, paired median
   −0.96), and the last improvement moves from evaluation 63 to 78, so the run is still descending at the end. Under
   every nonlinear g the low-noise arm ends where the default does (f⁴: 104 vs 88; log f: 10.9 vs 9.4; the only
   gain is f², −0.5 in 8/10 seeds), so its gaps are larger by exactly the amount its reference improved. On the
   compressing g the low-noise GP still fits noise of 10⁻²–10⁻¹: the bound is not binding there, the GP wants the noise.
5. **Ranks make BO exactly invariant, at a cost.** Proposals are bit-identical across all eight g, but on f itself
   rank-BO ends 2 orders of magnitude worse than default BO and 2.6 worse than the low-noise arm (worse in 10/10
   seeds). It stops improving at evaluation 27 (median). Its lengthscales (5.6, 1.2, 0.8, 0.6, 0.36) are ordered like
   the default GP's on f, so ranks keep the geometry; what they lose is resolution: the rank GP fits noise of 3·10⁻²,
   300× the default bound, because the staircase of ranks near the optimum looks like noise to an RBF kernel.
   Shirakawa (2016) reports the same cost on Branin (2.5× in value, 10⁴× in seed spread) and calls it not considerable.
6. **Two GP failure modes (`gp_fit.png`).** On f the default GP's lengthscales are 4.0, 2.2, 1.2, 0.6, 0.26, ordered
   like the ellipsoid's axis weights. Expanding g (f², f⁴) sends the lengthscales of dimensions 1–2 to 40–60 in
   normalised units, i.e. the GP ignores those axes, while the noise stays at the bound: confident and wrong.
   Compressing g (log f, f^0.25, f/(f+100)) shortens the lengthscales and raises the noise 100–700×: uncertain. The
   confident-and-wrong case gives the larger gap. The remaining error at BO's best point under f⁴ is spread over all
   five dimensions, so the lengthscales point at the mechanism without fully explaining the size of the gap.

**Choices to review.**
- Budgets: BO 90 evaluations (100 in the first run; the default arm's result at 90 equals its result at 100, 0.078
  vs 0.077); CMA-ES 2000 (H1's value).
- One saturating constant (c = 100); a second value, e.g. c = median f ≈ 1200, would give a mild case.
- Rank variant: plain ranks. Normal scores Φ⁻¹((r − ½)/n), or Shirakawa's Lebesgue-measure objective, are equally
  invariant and separate the best points more, which may reduce the cost in finding 5.
- Low-noise arm: bound 10⁻⁸ with BoTorch's LogNormal(−4, 1) noise prior kept. On f the fit went to 10⁻⁷, so the prior
  did not hold it up; a flat prior was not tried.
- LogEI only; H1 also ran EI.
- Inherited from H1: the CMA-ES start point is exactly x₀ = 2.5·x*, because both are drawn from the same seeded
  stream. Harmless for the paired gaps, but it matters for any absolute CMA-ES result. Fixing it changes H1 too.
  Also σ₀ = 2.5, whereas the outline said 3.
